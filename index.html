More than water

A river is not just a line on a map.

India's rivers carry stories, support farms, connect communities and shape our culture. Many are revered as sacred. Yet sewage, plastic, industrial waste and careless disposal continue to place these living ecosystems under pressure.

How can we call a river sacred and still allow it to become polluted? Respect must reach beyond rituals. It must show up in everyday choices, public systems and collective responsibility.

Uttarakhand to Bengal

A closer look • Yamuna

When a river is pushed beyond its limits.

In Delhi, the Yamuna receives pollution from multiple drains and untreated or inadequately treated wastewater. Official reporting in 2025 highlighted the scale of the challenge.

641 MLDEstimated sewage reported as untreated in Delhi in June 2025. MLD means million litres per day.
40 mg/LBiochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD) reported at Nizamuddin Bridge, compared with the bathing-water criterion of below 3 mg/L.

These are dated reported figures, not live readings. Conditions vary by location and time.

Progress takes persistence

Cleaning a river is a long-term commitment.

River restoration programmes are improving wastewater infrastructure and supporting conservation. Progress matters—but the work is not finished.

2014

Namami Gange launched

A national programme began to address pollution and support conservation and rejuvenation of the Ganga.

2025

Infrastructure expanded

Government reporting described 136 sewage infrastructure projects completed and operational, with 3,780 MLD treatment capacity.

Today

Keep the pressure on

Infrastructure, monitoring, enforcement and public participation must continue for lasting improvement.

Know what harms rivers

Pollution has many sources. So do solutions.

Understanding the causes helps us ask better questions and support the right changes.

01

Untreated sewage

Wastewater entering rivers without adequate treatment can harm water quality and aquatic life.

02

Plastic & solid waste

Discarded packaging and other waste travel downstream and damage habitats.

03

Industrial discharge

Poorly managed effluents can introduce harmful substances into waterways.

04

Runoff & encroachment

Polluted runoff and damaged wetlands weaken natural river systems.

Small actions. Shared impact.

Let respect become a daily habit.

River restoration needs strong public infrastructure and accountability. Individuals can help by reducing waste, keeping drains clear, supporting local clean-ups and reporting pollution through civic channels.
